## Context

Batch 6 of [`../features/ecs.md`](../features/ecs.md) is the benchmark and allocation check. The rules are fixed: with 1,000 objects × 4 traits, a step (queries, standard systems and flush) stays at or under 1 ms median on the desktop reference and allocates nothing in steady state (`REQ-ECS-009`); the desktop reference is the CI trend-job runner, a GitHub Actions standard Linux runner (ADR-0034); no benchmark library, an own headless harness with a warm-up and a median over many steps; CI asserts only zero steady-state allocation (per-thread allocation counter) and linear scaling of operation counts; wall-clock time is reported, never asserted, and a time budget is checked by a separate non-blocking trend job (ADR-0030, decision 3); pooled trait instances in dense arrays first, `Transformable` and `Movable` moved to primitive arrays only if the budget is missed (ADR-0030, decision 4); per-trait memory is measured by the benchmark batch and each standard trait documents its bytes per instance (ADR-0034); budgets and memory values per tier are measured, not guessed, and recorded in `testing.md` (`REQ-PERF-001`, ADR-0024, decision 2); the Android measurement comes with the DEX job (ADR-0030, ADR-0044). The feature page asks for 1,000 objects × 4 traits and a 10,000-object scaling check.

The standard traits and systems named by `REQ-ECS-009` belong to [`../features/standard-traits.md`](../features/standard-traits.md) and the `traits` module, which has no type yet. The documents leave open what the benchmark runs on until then, where the harness lives, its warm-up and step counts, what counts are compared for the scaling, how the benchmark is kept out of the blocking build, how the trend job runs it and where it reports, and how the memory is measured.

## Decision

1. **Stand-in scene (owner answer).** Until `traits` exists, the benchmark runs on game-defined stand-ins shaped like the standard traits and systems, in the `ecs` test sources (`BenchmarkWorld`): four traits per object, `Transformable` (center position, full size, previous position, m), `Movable` (velocity and maximum speed, m/s), `Gravity` (acceleration, negative Y down, m/s², and terminal velocity, a magnitude, m/s) and `Lifetime` (remaining duration, s), all `double` fields (ADR-0014); three queries iterated by the systems at the standard slots of ADR-0034, `Gravity` at 2000 (accelerates the velocity, never beyond the terminal velocity), `Movement` at 3000 (captures the previous position, clamps the speed, integrates the velocity) and `Lifetime` at 5000 (counts down and requests the removal on expiry, from inside the iteration); and a spawner at 6000 (late bookkeeping) creating one object with the four traits for each removal of the step, so the population stays constant. `Jump` and `Collision` are left out: they need `physics` and tiles, and four traits is the size of the requirement. Lifetimes depend on the creation rank only (1 to 100 steps for the first objects, 100 steps for the respawned ones), never on random draws or iteration order, so about 1 % of the objects are removed and created again each step and a world of ten times the objects does exactly ten times the work. The values are benchmark data, not engine defaults. When the standard traits and systems exist, the `traits` batches replace the stand-ins with them and measure again with this harness; the values recorded here stay labelled as stand-in values until then.
2. **Where the harness lives.** In the `ecs` test sources, package-private: `StepHarness` (measure and statistics), `BenchmarkWorld` (the scene) and `CountingType` (a trait type counting its pool creations and resets). Not in `test-support`, which depends on `runtime`, `platform-api` and `render-api` and on which `ecs` does not depend (ADR-0045): a dependency for one module's benchmark is not worth it. The classes ship in the `ecs` test JAR like every test class; a later batch that benchmarks another module on top of `ecs` (`traits`, `multiplayer`) makes the ones it needs public in that test JAR, the fixture placement of ADR-0045.
3. **Measure.** `StepHarness.measure(scene, warmup, steps)` runs the warm-up steps, then times each measured `World.update(stepSeconds)` (the systems, their queries and the flush) with `System.nanoTime()`, and summarizes the durations: the median (the mean of the two middle values for an even count), the 90th and 99th percentiles by nearest rank, the maximum and the mean. The benchmark (`StepBenchmarkTest`) measures 1,000 objects × 4 traits and 10,000 objects × 4 traits, each with 5,000 warm-up steps and 10,000 measured steps at 1/60 s, and reports the median ratio between them. The ratio is reported only: time depends on caches, so the scaling is asserted on counts (decision 6).
4. **Memory.** The benchmark also reports the bytes per instance of each stand-in trait (the per-thread allocation counter over 10,000 instances, divided by their number: the object header and the fields as laid out by the JVM) and the bytes allocated to build and populate a second world of 1,000 objects × 4 traits once the classes are loaded (tables, storages, pools, queries, systems and the growth from the default capacities). These are the values of `REQ-PERF-001` for the desktop row of `testing.md`. ADR-0034 asks each standard trait to document its bytes per instance in its Javadoc: that comes with each standard trait, measured with this harness; the stand-ins have no public Javadoc to carry it.
5. **Kept out of the blocking build.** The benchmark carries the JUnit tag `benchmark`. The default build and the `platform-tests` profile leave it out (`excludedGroups` of the aggregator `pom.xml`); the new profile `benchmark` runs only the tests of that tag and sets the system property `lionengine.benchmark.budget` to `true`, under which the benchmark asserts the median step at 1,000 objects at or under 1 ms (`REQ-ECS-009`); without the property it only reports. The `audio-device-tests` profile, which runs every test, runs it too and only reports. Command: `mvn -B -pl lionengine-ecs -am verify -Pbenchmark`.
6. **CI-asserted checks.** In every build: `BenchmarkAllocationTest`, the full step of the scene at 1,000 objects × 4 traits, 1,000 warm-up steps, then 1,000 measured steps allocating less than the 1 KiB tolerance of the other allocation tests, with no pool creation, no capacity growth and the population and query counts unchanged; and `BenchmarkScalingTest`, the scene at 1,000 and 10,000 objects, 200 warm-up and 500 measured steps, every operation count exactly ten times larger at 10,000: the objects visited through the three queries, the creations requested, the four flush events delivered to an object listener and a trait listener (one per effective change, so each command applied once), the resets of the pools, and no pool creation; the object capacity stays under twice the population plus its 1 % turnover (doubling growth).
7. **Trend job.** The job `benchmark` of `.github/workflows/build.yml`, on the pull requests and pushes of `rewrite/v11`, runs on `ubuntu-latest` (the desktop reference of ADR-0034) with `continue-on-error`, so a missed budget marks this job only and never blocks a merge. It runs the command of decision 5, appends the report `lionengine-ecs/target/benchmark-reports/ecs-step.md` (Markdown, also written to the build output through the engine `Log` at `INFO`) to the job summary, and uploads it as the artifact `benchmark-reports` of each run: the series of runs is the trend.
8. **Storage stays pooled.** The measured median at 1,000 objects × 4 traits on the reference runner (`testing.md`, "Measured budgets") is far under the 1 ms budget, so the traits stay pooled instances in dense arrays and `Transformable` and `Movable` do not move to primitive arrays (ADR-0030, decision 4). A later measure with the standard traits that misses the budget reopens it.
9. **Android.** The Android measurement is deferred: the DEX job of ADR-0044 runs the device tests on an x86_64 API 21 emulator, which is not the mid-range API 21-era reference device of `REQ-PERF-002`, and no document names that device or says how its benchmark is run (JUnit 6 runs on the JVM only, ADR-0050). Recorded as `OQ-177`; the Android row of `testing.md` stays "not measured yet". The harness, the scene and the tested `ecs` code use plain arrays and Java 21 APIs available on API 21; `ecs` main code is unchanged by this batch.

## Options considered

- Scene: stand-ins now, measured again with `traits` (chosen by the owner) vs deferring the batch until `traits` exists (no allocation and scaling check of the full step until then).
- Harness place: `ecs` test sources (chosen; no new dependency) vs a `test-support` helper (`ecs` would depend on the runtime and render contracts for its tests) vs a new benchmark module (a module for three classes).
- Out of the blocking build: a JUnit tag and a profile (chosen; the pattern of `platform` and `audio-device`) vs running the benchmark in every build and reporting only (time in every job, timing noise of busy runners) vs a separate main class run by the job (a second way to run tests).
- Budget check: an assertion under a system property set by the profile (chosen; one test, the job fails visibly) vs a script parsing the report (a new command and format to maintain).
- Scaling: counts the world produces (chosen; exact, cannot flake) vs a time ratio (measured here at about 15 to 20, from caches, not from the algorithm).
- Trend: job summary and per-run artifact (chosen; no store to run) vs a stored history in the repository (commits from CI).

## Consequences

- Positive consequences: the 1 ms budget of `REQ-ECS-009` and its zero allocation are measured on the full step, from the first ECS release; the allocation and scaling checks run in every build; the time budget is watched without making CI flaky.
- Costs / constraints: the values are those of stand-ins until `traits` exists; the trend is the list of job summaries and artifacts, with GitHub's retention; the Android value waits for `OQ-177`.
- Impact on modules and APIs: no public API change; `ecs` test sources gain `StepHarness`, `BenchmarkWorld`, `CountingType`, `StepBenchmarkTest`, `BenchmarkAllocationTest` and `BenchmarkScalingTest`; the aggregator `pom.xml` gains the tag exclusion and the profile `benchmark`; `build.yml` gains the job `benchmark`.
- Impact on tests and documentation: `features/ecs.md` (State, acceptance criteria), `features/standard-traits.md` (measured again with the standard traits), `features/README.md`, `traceability.md` (`REQ-ECS-009`, `REQ-PERF-001`, `REQ-PERF-002`), `testing.md` (tag, profile, measured budgets), `implementation-guide.md`, `README.md`, `AGENTS.md` (the command), `contracts.md`, `engine-features.md`, `roadmap.md`, `agent-workflow.md`, `open-questions.md` (`OQ-177`).
