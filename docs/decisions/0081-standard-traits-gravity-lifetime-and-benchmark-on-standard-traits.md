## Context

Batch 2 of [`../features/standard-traits.md`](../features/standard-traits.md) delivers `Gravity` and `Lifetime` with their systems. The rules are fixed: `Gravity` holds an acceleration in m/s², negative Y pulling down (Y up, ADR-0013), and a terminal velocity stored as a magnitude; its system accelerates `Movable` objects and never exceeds the terminal velocity (`REQ-STD-003`, ADR-0030, decision 7); `Lifetime` holds a duration in seconds and its system requests the removal of the object on expiry, applied at the flush (`REQ-STD-006`); slots `Gravity` 2000 and `Lifetime` 5000 (ADR-0034); `double` values (ADR-0014); registry ids `lionengine:gravity` and `lionengine:lifetime` with parameters named after the fields (ADR-0032); the form of batch 1 for traits and systems (ADR-0080: `TYPE` constants, private fields with accessors, systems created with the world and the storages, one query exposed by `getQuery()`, an array of excluded trait types, no allocation per step, bytes per instance measured by `TraitMemoryTest`, class names with the suffix `System`); and, by ADR-0079, the batch that delivers the last of `Transformable`, `Movable`, `Gravity` and `Lifetime` replaces the stand-ins of the `ecs` benchmark with them, measures again with `StepHarness` and records the values, the harness classes it needs made public in the `ecs` test JAR (ADR-0045) since `ecs` cannot depend on `traits`.

The documents leave open whether the acceleration is a Y scalar or a vector, what the terminal velocity limits (the component along the acceleration or the whole speed), whether the `Gravity` system needs a `Transformable`, whether a lifetime is a remaining duration or an initial duration with an elapsed time, whether it expires at `0` or below `0`, the reset states, whether an expired object is removed once, whether the stand-in scene stays, and whether the benchmark scene includes the previous-position capture.

## Decision

1. **Acceleration (owner answer).** A vertical scalar, `Gravity.getAcceleration()` in m/s², negative pulls down, positive pulls up, `0` pulls nowhere; no horizontal component.
2. **Terminal velocity (owner answer).** A magnitude, positive or zero, limiting the vertical velocity in the direction of the acceleration only: each step the system computes `vy + acceleration * stepSeconds`; with a negative acceleration, a result below `-terminalVelocity` becomes `min(vy, -terminalVelocity)`, with a positive one a result above `+terminalVelocity` becomes `max(vy, +terminalVelocity)`. So the gravity never pushes the vertical velocity past the limit, and a vertical velocity already beyond it (set by the game: a dash, a dive) is neither cut nor increased. The horizontal velocity is never changed. `Movable.maxSpeed` still clamps the whole speed afterwards in `MovementSystem` (ADR-0080, decision 2).
3. **Processed objects (owner answer).** `GravitySystem` has one query including `Movable` and `Gravity` (and none of the excluded types): an object with a `Gravity` and no `Movable` is skipped; no `Transformable` is needed, since the gravity changes only the velocity.
4. **Lifetime count (owner answer).** `Lifetime` holds the **remaining** duration, in seconds, counted down in place: each step `LifetimeSystem` subtracts the step, then requests `World.remove(id)` when the remaining duration is **at or under `0`**. The removal, with every trait, is applied by the flush ending the step: systems after slot 5000 still see the object in that step, the next step no longer does, so the removal is requested once and the object never counted again. With the constant step `dt` an object of duration `d` expires at about `ceil(d / dt)` steps, one more when the `double` subtraction leaves a tiny positive remainder; the tests compute the expected step by the same subtraction.
5. **Reset states (owner answer for `Lifetime`; same rule as `Movable` for `Gravity`).** A new or reset `Lifetime` never expires (`Double.POSITIVE_INFINITY`); a new or reset `Gravity` has no acceleration (`0`) and no terminal velocity (`Double.POSITIVE_INFINITY`), so an unset trait changes nothing. `setTerminalVelocity` and `setDuration` refuse a negative value and `NaN` (`IllegalArgumentException`), like `Movable.setMaxSpeed`; a duration of `0` expires at the next step; the acceleration is not checked (the game keeps it finite, like the velocity).
6. **Benchmark scene (owner answer).** The stand-ins are replaced in place: the scene, `StepBenchmarkTest`, `BenchmarkAllocationTest` and `BenchmarkScalingTest` move from the `ecs` test sources to the `traits` test sources and run on the standard traits and five systems, as a game registers them: `TransformCaptureSystem`, `GravitySystem`, `MovementSystem`, `LifetimeSystem` and the spawner at 6000; the stand-in scene is removed. `StepHarness` (now `measure(world, stepSeconds, warmup, steps)`) and `CountingType` (now a wrapper counting the creations and resets of another `TraitType`, wrapping the `TYPE` constants) are public in the `ecs` test JAR, on which `traits` depends in test scope. The spawner knows how many objects expire in each step without walking them: each duration is a whole number of steps counted down by the subtraction of `LifetimeSystem`, so its expiry step is computed at creation and counted in a ring of due steps. The command is `mvn -B -pl lionengine-traits -am verify -Pbenchmark`; the report is `lionengine-traits/target/benchmark-reports/step.md`; the trend job runs that command and publishes that directory.
7. **Accessors and names.** `Gravity`: `getAcceleration`, `setAcceleration(acceleration)`, `getTerminalVelocity`, `setTerminalVelocity(terminalVelocity)`; `Lifetime`: `getDuration`, `setDuration(duration)` (the remaining duration); the field names match the parameter names of ADR-0032 (`acceleration`, `terminalVelocity`, `duration`). The acceleration step and the count-down are package-private operations of the traits used by the systems. `GravitySystem(world, movables, gravities[, excluded])` and `LifetimeSystem(world, lifetimes[, excluded])`, with `SLOT` 2000 and 5000 and `getQuery()`. No factory for `lionengine:gravity` and `lionengine:lifetime` yet (`OQ-178`).
8. **Bytes per instance.** Measured by `TraitMemoryTest`: 32 bytes per `Gravity` and 24 per `Lifetime` on the desktop JVM (64-bit HotSpot, compressed class pointers), the values of their stand-ins, written in their Javadoc and asserted as upper bounds.
9. **Allocation.** No allocation per step in either system; `GravityLifetimeAllocationTest` asserts it over 1,000 steps of 1,000 objects with one expiry and one creation per step, and `BenchmarkAllocationTest` over the full step of the scene, with the 1 KiB tolerance.
10. **Storage stays pooled.** The benchmark measured again with the standard traits stays far under the 1 ms budget of `REQ-ECS-009` (`testing.md`, "Measured budgets"), so the traits stay pooled instances and `Transformable` and `Movable` do not move to primitive arrays (ADR-0030, decision 4; ADR-0079, decision 8).

## Options considered

- Acceleration: Y scalar (chosen: what "negative Y pulls down" describes, one field) vs a 2D vector (side gravity, a second field and a projection for the limit).
- Terminal velocity: along the acceleration (chosen: a fall stops speeding up, a dash or a jump keeps its speed) vs the whole speed (duplicates `maxSpeed`, slows a fast horizontal run).
- Processed objects: `Movable` and `Gravity` (chosen: no data needed beyond the velocity) vs also `Transformable` (a third trait for no use).
- Lifetime: remaining duration counted down, expiry at or under `0`, never by default (chosen) vs an initial duration and an elapsed time (two fields, the initial value readable) vs expiry under `0` only (a duration of exactly one step would last two) vs `0` by default (an unset lifetime removes the object at the first step).
- Benchmark: replace the stand-ins with five systems (chosen: the values are those of a game) vs four systems without the capture vs keeping both scenes (two sets of values to maintain).

## Consequences

- Positive consequences: falling objects and timed objects with no game code; the `REQ-ECS-009` benchmark measures the real standard traits and systems; the `ecs` test sources keep only the harness.
- Costs / constraints: an object pulled by gravity needs a `Movable`; a game reading a lifetime after expiry sees `0` or less until the flush; the benchmark now runs from the `traits` module.
- Impact on modules and APIs: `traits` gains `Gravity`, `Lifetime`, `GravitySystem`, `LifetimeSystem`; its test scope depends on the `ecs` test JAR; `ecs` test sources lose the stand-in scene and its three tests, `StepHarness` and `CountingType` become public; the aggregator `pom.xml` comment, `build.yml` (job `benchmark`), `README.md`, `AGENTS.md` and `implementation-guide.md` name the new command; `examples` gains `FallExample` and `Fall`; the Android `TraitsTest` covers the two traits.
- Impact on tests and documentation: `features/standard-traits.md`, `features/ecs.md`, `features/README.md`, `contracts.md`, `api-index.md`, `engine-features.md`, `roadmap.md`, `agent-workflow.md`, `traceability.md`, `testing.md`, `android-compatibility.md`.
